BetterConnections

Understand your communication style — and how to connect with the four types of people you work with every day.

Why This Matters

Most workplace friction isn't about the work. It's about communication style.

Every person you work with — your manager, your team, a hiring manager — has a dominant style that shapes how they receive information, make decisions, and build trust. When you know their style, you stop guessing and start connecting.

Personality Quiz

What's your communication style?

8 quick questions. No right or wrong answers. Find out if you're a Driver, Analytical, Expressive, or Amiable — and what that means for how you work.

🔴

Driver

Decisive. Direct. Results-focused.

🔵

Analytical

Thorough. Data-driven. Precise.

🟣

Expressive

Energetic. Inspiring. Big-picture.

🟢

Amiable

Supportive. Steady. People-first.

Question 1 of 8

🔥
Hot Buttons — What motivates each style Driver · Analytical · Expressive · Amiable
▼
🔴 Driver
⚡

Lead with outcomes

Skip the backstory. Start with the result, the deadline, and the decision needed. Drivers want to know: what are we solving and when does it need to be done?

🎯

Respect their time

Be concise. Give two clear options rather than open-ended questions. They'll decide fast — your job is to make the decision easy.

🏆

Show confidence

Drivers respect people who own their position. Don't hedge or over-explain. Make your case and stand behind it.

🔵 Analytical
📊

Provide facts and data

Analyticals don't trust claims without evidence. Back up your points with numbers, sources, or step-by-step logic. Vague assertions make them skeptical.

🔍

Explain the why

They want to understand the reasoning, not just the conclusion. Walk them through your process — they'll trust the outcome more when they can follow the logic.

⏳

Give them time to think

Don't pressure for an immediate answer. Analyticals make better decisions when they can process carefully. Follow up in writing so they can review.

🟣 Expressive
🎉

Bring energy and stories

Expressives connect through enthusiasm and narrative. Open with a story or an exciting vision before getting into the details. Energy is contagious to them.

💡

Make it social and fun

They thrive when there's collaboration, laughter, and creativity in the room. Dry, transactional communication shuts them down. Make them feel part of something.

🚀

Paint the vision

Show them the big picture before the details. Expressives are motivated by possibility and impact — give them the "what could be" before the "how."

🟢 Amiable
🤝

Build trust first

Amiables need to feel safe before they engage fully. Take a moment to connect personally before getting to business. Relationships come before transactions.

👂

Invite their input

They often won't speak up unless asked. Create space for their perspective — "What do you think?" goes a long way. They have more to offer than they show.

🕊️

Keep the tone kind

Blunt or aggressive communication shuts Amiables down immediately. They'll disengage rather than push back. Warmth isn't weakness — it's the key to the relationship.

🔗
Compatibility — Who clicks, who clashes Best matches · Challenging pairs · Why it matters at work
▼

Understanding who you naturally click with — and who creates friction — gives you a real edge in interviews, team dynamics, and working with managers.

🔴

Driver — Best with: Amiable. Hard with: Expressive.

Drivers and Amiables balance each other — the Driver provides direction, the Amiable provides harmony. Driver + Expressive clashes because both want the spotlight and neither wants to slow down for the other.

🔵

Analytical — Best with: Driver. Hard with: Amiable.

Analytical + Driver works because the Driver sets the direction and the Analytical builds the plan. Analytical + Amiable struggles because neither pushes decisions forward — both wait for the other to lead.

🟣

Expressive — Best with: Amiable. Hard with: Analytical.

Expressive + Amiable creates high warmth and energy. Expressive + Analytical grates — the Expressive finds the Analytical cold and slow; the Analytical finds the Expressive unfocused and loud.

🟢

Amiable — Best with: Expressive. Hard with: Driver.

Amiable + Expressive bond easily over warmth and collaboration. Amiable + Driver is the hardest pair — the Driver's bluntness feels harsh to the Amiable; the Amiable's pace feels slow to the Driver.

Interview Application

Reading your interviewer's style

Driver interviewer

Gets to the point fast, asks about results and timelines. Match their pace. Keep answers concise — use numbers and outcomes. Don't ramble.

Analytical interviewer

Asks detailed process questions. Show your methodology. Walk through your thinking step by step. Have data ready.

Expressive interviewer

Warm and conversational, may go off-topic. Match their energy. Tell stories. Show enthusiasm for the role and the team.

Amiable interviewer

Focuses on team fit and how you work with others. Lead with collaboration and examples of supporting teammates. Avoid coming across as aggressive or dismissive of others.

Prompt Formula

Role + Context + Goal + Standard + Format

The clearer your direction, the more useful the first draft becomes. This formula works for any AI tool — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini.

Role

Tell AI what expert to be. "Act as a senior healthcare contract manager..."

Context

What situation are you in? What constraints matter?

Goal

What specific outcome do you need?

Standard

What does excellent output look like? Define the quality bar before you start.

Format

How should the output be delivered? Bullets, table, email, one-pager?

Guided Practice

Build your own progression

Step 1: Write a basic prompt you commonly use.

Step 2: Rewrite it with defined outcome, constraints, and audience.

Step 3: Convert it into a repeatable system or template.

The goal is not longer prompts. The goal is structured thinking that produces repeatable results.
